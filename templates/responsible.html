Responsible data use

Support, not labels.
Support, not punishment.

Learning analytics can help, but only when used carefully. These are the limits and promises of our prototype.

“Support, not labels. Support, not punishment.”

Small hypothetical dataset

The student data has only five made-up records and the module data is synthetic. Any pattern we see may just be chance.

Experimental scoring weights

The 25/30/20/25 weights and the friction index weights were chosen by us. Different weights would give different results.

No validated predictive model

LearnSense AI is rule-based. We have not trained a model and we do not claim any prediction accuracy.

Privacy and informed consent

Real learners should know what is collected, why, and who sees it. They should be able to say no.

Data minimisation

Only collect what is needed. The prototype uses five numbers per session and no names, faces or personal details.

Potential bias

Slow typing, a poor internet connection, a different first language or a disability can all look like “friction” even when understanding is strong.

Misleading interpretations

High activity does not always mean understanding, and a low quiz does not mean low ability. Numbers describe one moment only.

Need for diverse learner testing

Before real use the rules must be tested with learners of different backgrounds, courses and abilities.

Teacher oversight

A teacher should always review a suggestion before it reaches a learner. The tool supports teachers and does not replace them.

Student feedback

Learners should be able to say whether a suggestion helped, and that feedback should be used to improve the rules.
